## Chapter 1 - Planet Landing, Performance Failures

Dr. Bharadwaj once told Murderbot she thought it hated planets. Murderbot's response - internal, not said aloud - is that Bharadwaj was not wrong. The setting is a planet where conditions are difficult, and the ongoing context is that Murderbot has been experiencing performance failures: unscheduled shutdowns, fragmented archives, threat assessment that goes missing at critical moments. The alien remnant contamination from Mural was removed, but something in its cognitive architecture is not recovering the way it should.

Murderbot is here with a Preservation mission team: **Iris** (lead researcher), **Ratthi**, **Tarik** (planetary geologist), and **ART-drone** - a drone controlled by ART, who cannot be physically present but is maintaining remote connection to both the drone's systems and Murderbot's feed. Their mission: contact a pre-Corporation Rim colonist group called **AdaCol2**, located in a buried installation that has been sealed and cut off from the outside for generations, and help them understand their options before a Barish-Estranza team arrives to do the same thing with corporate persuasion.

---

## Chapter 2 - Scanning AdaCol2's Installation

Concealed behind an outcrop in the bad planetary weather, Murderbot does a scan of the approaches to AdaCol2's installation. The installation has sealed exterior hatches, its own power generation, and multiple camera positions that have not been active in decades. The colonists know something is out there. The team has to find a way in that does not look like a threat - which is harder when the team includes a SecUnit.

---

## Chapter 3 - Team Disagreement, B-E Timeline

An argument among the group. Not everyone agrees on the approach. The central disagreement: whether to make contact openly through the installation's communication systems, or to wait until the Barish-Estranza team arrives and try to intervene then. Murderbot notes, internally, that this is what humans call a discussion. What Murderbot calls it is less polite.

ART-drone contributes analysis on the B-E team's probable timeline. The B-E team is led by **Leonide**, a corporate officer who is good at her job. Getting inside AdaCol2 before she does is the only version of this that does not end with the colonists signing a B-E contract.

---

## Chapter 4 - Underground Tunnels, First Contact

Hatchways down into underground tunnels. The team finds a construction access route into AdaCol2's lower levels that was sealed from the inside but not permanently: the colonists used it for maintenance access. Getting it open without triggering AdaCol2's internal sensors takes longer than expected. Murderbot goes in first while ART-drone manages the sensor question from outside.

Once inside, the installation is old. Very old. The Pre-CR colonists originally built for permanent survival, which means their infrastructure is over-engineered by modern standards and has kept running despite centuries of minimal maintenance. The temperature is stable. The air is clean. Somewhere above them, people live here who have never seen the outside sky.

---

## Chapter 5 - AdaCol2 Entrance, Contact Made

Close enough to the inhabited zone to see its entrance: a large hatchway set into the structure's rock substrate, sealed from the colony side. Murderbot scouts ahead with drones while the team waits. The colonists do have functional camera systems inside the inhabited zone, though most of the external cameras have been dark for a long time. Before opening the hatch, Murderbot wants to understand what is on the other side. It maps the immediate interior via drone feed: a corridor leading to a transit area, then a larger communal space.

The colonists detect the drone. Contact is made. **AdaCol2** - the installation's governing system - is aware of their presence and has been monitoring since they entered the construction access. It is not hostile. It is, in fact, cautiously curious. It knew someone would come eventually.

---

## Chapter 6 - Colonist Council, Separatist Faction

AdaCol2's human council does not react uniformly to the news that a Preservation team is here. The separatist faction - colonists who have structured their entire identity around the belief that they owe nothing to the outside world and do not want to be incorporated into any governing structure - are deeply skeptical. The non-separatist majority is frightened: they have been told about Barish-Estranza, they know the Corporation Rim's record with isolated settlements, and they do not want to end up under corporate charter. But they don't fully trust Preservation either.

Murderbot presents the Preservation mission honestly - no contracts, no coercion, a genuine offer of governance by an alliance that does not treat humans as property - and is believed less than Iris presenting the same information, because Iris is a human and Murderbot is a SecUnit, and the colonists know what SecUnits are used for.

---

## Chapter 7 - B-E Shuttle Arrives, Meeting Agreed

The Barish-Estranza shuttle arrives. Leonide's team requests access through AdaCol2's communications. The colonists, who have already been talking to the Preservation team for two days, do not give immediate assent. This is not what Leonide expected.

ART-drone begins making a case to the colonists using archived legal and historical documentation. A meeting is agreed: Leonide and her team will present B-E's offer, and the colonists will hear both sides before making any decision. Iris agrees. The meeting is set for the following day. Murderbot notes that Leonide is very good, and that the separatist faction is still a problem, and that B-E having a SecUnit on their team is going to be a factor.

---

## Chapter 8 - The Documentary, Ratthi's Message

Working through the night in their assigned rooms in the installation, Murderbot and ART-drone build a documentary. Not a corporate presentation - a piece of media, an actual story. It uses interview footage from the labor refugees rescued in Fugitive Telemetry, excerpts from Dr. Bharadwaj's documentary on indenture in the Corporation Rim, data from mining and work colonies, and a fictionalized narrative Murderbot constructs around the contract workers it traveled with before arriving at AdaCol2. The workers they had met on the ship en route who had thought Murderbot was an augmented human consultant, who were being shipped to a work camp and knew their future was bad but tried not to think about it.

Ratthi revises the narration. He has a gift for making things clear and making people care. Murderbot tells him this indirectly, by sending him the outline to revise rather than doing it itself. Ratthi tells Murderbot - through text, not out loud - that it's the right person to write this. Murderbot archives the message for later.

---

## Chapter 9 - Meeting Leonide, Threat Assessment

The meeting room in AdaCol2 is smaller than the ball-sport chamber, its walls pale gray, its padded seats obviously colonist-made additions to the Pre-CR frame. Murderbot places two drones inconspicuously before Leonide's team enters. It offers the video feed to AdaCol2, which adds the meeting to its live broadcast to the colonists. This is deliberate: the colonists watching will see the corporate team's behavior in real time.

Leonide is unfazed. She recognizes the camera broadcast for what it is and acknowledges it with something close to admiration. The meeting is a sustained exercise in two people who are very good at their respective things operating in the same room. Leonide is prepared for the documentary Murderbot made and treats it as a "sales pitch" - a framing Iris rejects. Murderbot's threat assessment pings during the meeting: Leonide's support staff are showing abnormal muscle tension, out of sync with the conversation. Not nerves. Something else.

Before Murderbot can determine what the abnormality means, ART-drone receives the alert and the situation accelerates. Murderbot was right to worry.

---

## Chapter 10 - Dark Corridor Escape, B-E Schism

Tarik catches up with the rest of the group in the corridor junction leading to the powered-down section. They are moving fast, with **Leonide** - who is now cooperating, albeit warily - and without B-E's separatist support staff, who have been disarmed. The schism in B-E's management that Iris suspected turns out to be real: a faction angry about potential bonus loss due to mission failure had been prepared to act in ways that Leonide did not sanction and did not know about. Leonide, injured and irritated, acknowledges this under pressure.

Moving through the dark cargo bay - no power, no working emergency lighting, Murderbot navigating from stored map data while the humans use hand lights pointed at the floor - they are heading for the construction access exit. Murderbot has a projectile hole in its suit from the confrontation with the rogue B-E operatives but does not address it, citing distance and available energy reserves.

---

## Chapter 11 - Pseudohopper Flight, Ratthi's Survival Tent

The pseudohopper - a pre-CR-era tunnel vehicle - gets them out of the installation faster than the tunnel vehicle did on the way in. Tarik and Leonide share the controls. Murderbot sits and partially shuts down to recover its power reserve. ART-drone is also significantly damaged. Murderbot runs ART-drone's favorite episode of *World Hoppers* in their shared processing space and can tell it is watching.

Iris patches bloodstains off ART-drone's carapace. Leonide, exhausted, notices the media is playing in the feed and says nothing useful. Tarik deadpans that he always watches entertainment when he flies. Murderbot agrees with him.

At the tunnel exit, they find the bay blocked. Then they identify the obstruction: their shuttle, camouflaged under Ratthi's survival tent, which from above looks exactly like a dust drift. The plan worked. Ratthi's voice comes over comm, normal and reassuring. Murderbot notes the small spike in its performance reliability from hearing that Ratthi is safe, and considers how to feel about that, and then does not.

The colonists of AdaCol2, having watched the full meeting and the subsequent events via their own feed, have reached a decision.

## What This Book Does

System Collapse is the only Murderbot book that treats Murderbot's psychological damage as a central narrative problem rather than background context. The "system collapse" of the title is not a mechanical failure; it is what happens when a being that has spent decades not being allowed to have an interior life finally has one, and discovers that having an interior life includes having things go wrong with it.

The documentary Murderbot builds in Chapter Eight is the key action of the book: it is the first time Murderbot explicitly uses what it has learned from media - the way stories make people care about strangers - to try to do something good for people it will never meet. Ratthi's message that it is "absolutely the right person to write this" is left in the archive for later. Murderbot doesn't know yet if it believes it. But it keeps the message.

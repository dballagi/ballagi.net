## Chapter 1 — Leaving the Kagala, Next Destination

The group leaves the Kagala behind, the ascension raft flying low over the dry plain. Everyone who survived Witch King is aboard or accounted for: Kai, Ziede, Tahren, Dahin, Sanja, **Tenes** (a young person who can speak to earth spirits), and **Ramad** (the Immortal Blessed historian). **Arnsterath** — the other Saredi demon revealed at the end of Witch King — is with them, her status complicated: not quite captive, not quite ally.

The immediate destination is somewhere Dahin has been leading toward, and the direction has the feel of an omission. The group is managing the aftermath of the Summer Halls: physically battered, politically exposed, unsure how much the Blessed Lands know about what happened, and aware that the Rising World faction still exists and still has resources.

---

## Chapter 2 — Dahin's Hidden Knowledge, Second Well

Kai settles in with cushions and tries to rest. Dahin's knowledge of the Hierarchs' second Well — a death well other than the Well of Thosaren, one whose location he has apparently known for some time — is becoming harder for him to keep compartmentalized. The question of why he hasn't told anyone about it, and what he intended to do with it, is the chapter's undertow.

The Well of Thosaren was the Hierarchs' primary source of power: a naturally formed concentration of death energy that the Hierarchs tapped to fuel their expositors and amalgams across enormous distances. If there is a second Well — as old records suggest — it would be equally capable of powering a new regime, if someone knew how to consecrate themselves to it as a Hierarch.

---

## Chapter 3 — The Tor Theory Revealed, Group Commits

Dahin's theory, finally stated clearly: the Hierarchs had a second Well, hidden in the hills of Sun-Ar, camouflaged as a natural rock formation — a tor. The theory is not new to him; he has been working on it for years. Ziede's response to this revelation is immediate and declarative. The word "later" does a lot of work in this chapter: there are things that need to happen before Dahin and Ziede finish their conversation.

Kai absorbs the information. If the Well exists and the Rising World finds it, the entire political structure the post-Hierarch world has built will be at risk. The group commits to finding it first.

---

## Chapter 4 — Traveling to Sun-Ar, Arnsterath in Tow

Kai wakes to Dahin leaning over him, asking whether he meant something he apparently said before losing consciousness. The chapter moves between recovery and planning — the two activities that occupy the same space in this story. Travel toward the tor in Sun-Ar requires crossing territory that is not friendly to their particular combination of beings. Ramad's contacts are useful for this.

Arnsterath continues to be a problem and a complication: present, monitored, occasionally cooperative, occasionally threatening. Kai's history with her is ancient and painful, and he deals with it by not dealing with it, which Ziede notes is typical.

---

## Chapter 5 — Belithan Scholars, Highsun Appears

Kai and Dahin find their way to the ramps leading out of wherever they have been staying in the Belithan scholars' territory. **Belithan scholars** are archivists who specialize in Hierarch-era documents and whose political neutrality is genuine rather than strategic. They have been a resource for Dahin's research. Getting access to their records has illuminated the tor's location and confirmed that something significant is embedded in it.

**Highsun** — an Immortal Blessed with a wanderer's history and a scholar's interest in Hierarch-era sites — appears in the scholars' circle. He has been mapping Hierarch-related sites independently, and his data overlaps significantly with Dahin's. His motivation is presented as academic. Kai's threat assessment of him is cautious.

---

## Chapter 6 — Flying to Sun-Ar, Dahin's Preparation

The ascension raft flies low over Ancartre (a city or significant settlement) on the way north and east toward Sun-Ar. The journey covers territory that looks post-war on the surface and carries the war underneath: abandoned roads, ruined relay stations, settlements built into the shells of pre-Hierarch infrastructure. Kai watches the ground unroll below and thinks about what it would mean to have this all undone.

Dahin has been increasingly quiet. Ziede knows what this means and watches him. Kai knows what it means and also watches him. Dahin appears to be preparing himself for something he has not announced.

---

## Chapter 7 — Final Research, Tor Confirmed

Back in the workroom the Belithan scholars have designated for the group's use. The final research synthesis before departure for the tor. What they know: the tor in the hills on the edge of Sun-Ar territory is artificial, built to look natural, pre-Hierarch in construction, probably Pre-Empire altogether. The Well beneath it is real and was used during the Hierarch period as a secondary source of power, with the location kept secret even from most Hierarchs. Only the Hierarch core and their most senior expositors knew of it. The records Dahin found are fragments of a destroyed archive that probably should not have survived.

Highsun continues to offer assistance. Kai continues to not quite trust him but to see no way to exclude him that wouldn't require a confrontation that would cost more than it gained.

---

## Chapter 8 — First View of the Tor, Kai Enters Alone

First clear view of the tor from the hilltop: larger than expected, its manufactured nature visible at close range in the stone slabs supporting its base. At the far end of the valley, the riding oxen from the scholarly expedition that went in earlier are wandering loose and still saddled — their riders did not return. Someone is in the tor who captured the scholars and is not concerned about being found.

Kai goes in alone. He wants to assess what they're dealing with before committing everyone. He finds the Well chamber: a large underground space with a platform, and the Well itself — a concentration of death energy so old and large it is almost geological. He gets too close. The Well "crushes" him — not physically, but in the way that something with that much death in it can crush a being who is still partly of the underearth. He falls.

---

## Chapter 9 — Kai Recovers, Dahin's Secret Plan

Kai wakes in flashes of awareness to Dahin's voice, and then is riding on horseback with someone's arm around him to keep him from falling. In his half-conscious state he says Bashasa's name. The voice that answers is Ramad's. This embarrasses Ramad considerably more than it does Kai.

When Kai fully wakes, it is daylight, he is in a tent at the Belithan scholars' camp, and all the scholars are alive: the people in the tor had captured but not killed them. The Well's power had kept Kai down — he sustained significant internal damage. Ziede's description of how she responded to his condition is not gentle.

Dahin comes in. He waited for Kai to wake because he has something to tell them both together. The full account of what Dahin knew, when he knew it, and what he intended to do when the group reached the tor: Dahin had found the records about the Well's one known vulnerability — if a willing Immortal Blessed sacrifices their own life to a death well, it supposedly destroys the well. He had come here intending to do this himself, without telling anyone, because he knew no one would let him if they knew.

---

## Chapter 10 — Highsun's Offer, Return to the Tor

Tahren, who has known Dahin for a long time, reacts to the news of his secret plan with the expression of someone who narrowly avoided a disaster they feel personally responsible for not preventing. The chapter assembles the surviving group: Highsun arrives and reveals he overheard enough of the previous conversation to understand the situation. He offers to join the effort to deal with the Well. He is accepted.

The plan: go back into the tor. Clear whoever is still inside. **Tenes** can communicate with the stone and may be able to find a secondary entrance, so they don't have to go through the front door where the Well's influence is strongest. Kai's ankles have knit back together sufficiently that he can walk.

Ramad, waiting outside the tent, is careful not to show what is on his face. Kai notes this and does not make it easy. Sanja, wrapped in an oversized coat, runs up and breaks the tension.

---

## Chapter 11 — Dark Well Chamber, Highsun Stopped

Inside the tor. The Well chamber is dark — all the firepots have been extinguished. There is one light source: Highsun's Immortal Blessed glow, near the center of the platform. Highsun is not where they expected him to be. He has gotten there before them, through his own means, and he has been using archived Hierarch records to understand how consecration to a Well works. He is attempting to make himself a Hierarch.

Kai's assessment, in the dark doorway: if Highsun already knows how to draw on the Well, they are all dead, so there is no point in doing anything. If he doesn't know yet, they have a chance. He sounds out the situation through the sound of paper — Highsun is still reading — and decides the chance is real.

The plan: Arnsterath will serve as a distraction, using her presence and her demonic confrontational capability to pull Highsun's attention to the right side of the chamber while Kai approaches from the left, toward the steps up to the platform. Tenes cannot help directly — she is too spent to speak to the stone — but she stays to assist Arnsterath if needed.

Arnsterath's agreement is preceded by genuine fear, disguised as negotiation. She is afraid of Kai. She is also afraid of what happens if Highsun succeeds, which fear is larger. She goes.

Kai goes the other way. The Well is still crushing, even at the edges of the chamber, but it is the underearth part of him it works on, and he is practiced at ignoring what tries to destroy him. He gets close. He kills Highsun before the consecration completes. The Well loses its would-be Hierarch.

The chamber stabilizes. Tenes finds the weakness Dahin identified in the structure and signals to the others at the entrance. Whether the Well is destroyed, sealed, or simply deprived of a master for the foreseeable future — and what Dahin's abandoned plan means for whatever comes next — is the question the book closes on, uneasily, with the sense that there is more of this world's story still to find.

## The Past Sections

*Queen Demon, like Witch King, alternates present-day chapters with historical flashbacks. These "The Past:" sections are set during the war against the Hierarchs — specifically in the period before the assault on the Summer Halls, when Bashasa's coalition army is on the march and working to build alliances. Each section is interleaved between present-day chapters in the following reading order: Spark (before Ch1), Fire (after Ch1), Hazard (after Ch2), Offer (after Ch3), Choice (after Ch4), Prelude (after Ch5), Testament (after Ch6), Trap (after Ch7), Dawn (after Ch8), Conflagration (after Ch9), Pledge (after Ch11).*

---

## The Past: the Spark — The Origin of the Rising World

*(Before Chapter 1)*

A historical record explaining the origin of the name "**Rising World**" — the movement and philosophy that is the antagonist in both Witch King and Queen Demon. The name was bestowed at some point during or after the war, and this section establishes its origins and initial meaning: a belief that the world could be restored and elevated, drawing on the desire to rebuild after the Hierarchs' devastation. The irony that this hopeful name now belongs to a faction attempting to revive Hierarch-style power is noted.

---

## The Past: the Fire — The Arike Cavalry's Unexpected Strength

*(After Chapter 1, before Chapter 2)*

A historical record noting that it was not initially understood how much stronger the Arike cavalry was than anyone — including the Hierarchs' intelligence — had predicted. The section documents a specific engagement where the Arike mounted forces proved decisive. In the coalition army's march toward the south, the cavalry's capabilities change the tactical calculations and contribute directly to the coalition's survival through territory that would otherwise have been impassable.

---

## The Past: the Hazard — The First Great War, Demons and the Underearth

*(After Chapter 2, before Chapter 3)*

A historical record about the **first great war** — not the Hierarch war, but the earlier conflict between demons and mortals that predates everything else in the known history. This is the war that Grandmother fought and ended by treaty, referenced in Witch King but not described. The section establishes that demon-mortal relations in the underearth were not always peaceful, and that the treaty Grandmother negotiated was a genuine peace at a genuine cost. It contextualizes Kai's existence: he is a product of a peace that was achieved through warfare.

---

## The Past: the Offer — The Dustwitch Attack, Captured Hawkmoth

*(After Chapter 3, before Chapter 4)*

Opens with a historical note about Benais-arik under Hierarch occupation: the city's canals and infrastructure in disarray, orphans housed in the Old Palace. The main action: Kai returns to the coalition army's camp after a **dustwitch attack** at night. Several vanguarders have been injured — one, **Amabel**, was struck with a magical cantrip designed to stop their heart. The cantrip was released before it could kill, either because the caster died first or as deliberate cruelty.

Ziede explains dustwitches to Kai's cadre: they are northern Witches driven south by the Hierarchs. Unlike Ziede's wind-spirit working, dustwitches draw power from the geological — rockfalls, seismic activity, the kinetic energy of the earth itself. They were able to confuse Ziede's wind-devils by mimicking the frequency of geological forces. The coalition has captured one dustwitch, **Hawkmoth**, and Kai and Ziede go to interrogate her.

---

## The Past: the Choice — The Debate Over Alliance, Bashasa Insists

*(After Chapter 4, before Chapter 5)*

Opens with a note about the Hierarchs' **pain well network** — how they established chains of small death wells down through the south by slaughtering city populations, creating distributed sources of power. The main action: almost the entire leadership of Bashasa's army is in the main room of the caravanserai, arguing.

Bashasa has released Hawkmoth on his own authority and wants to offer the dustwitches a formal alliance. Everyone else has objections. **Lahshar** — Bashasa's most persistent skeptic among the Prince-heirs — presents evidence that dustwitches have been attacking caravans in the area for years (bodies with dirt-choked mouths, witnesses from north Seidel). Tahren challenges her: "Is this fact or story?" The fact turns out to be accurate, which Kai finds unpleasant.

**Amabel** explains in Witchspeak (Kai translates): dustwitches don't operate with leaders the way the Arike do. They don't give orders; they respect elders and speak in counsel. Whether the **Doyen** (the dustwitch leader) can actually make binding decisions is unknowable. The room receives this with dismay.

**Hiranan**, the oldest and most experienced Prince-heir, needs to be convinced before the plan can move forward. Bashasa can't actually answer her concerns — so they take a break. He sits beside Kai. Kai has been collecting **luck tokens** that soldiers keep leaving on his folded coat — small metal figures that Bashasa explains are not luck exactly but cynosures, tokens of affiliation and belonging. Bashasa asks Kai: *Will you speak on my behalf? Not to the council. To the dustwitches.*

---

## The Past: the Prelude — Finding the Dustwitch Camp, Tahren and Dahin

*(After Chapter 5, before Chapter 6)*

Opens with a note from the City Archives at Seidel-arik: little is now known about the Witch King's origins, but this wasn't always true — the coalition's leadership would have known them well.

The main action: **Manah** and **Nadoch** (vanguarders) work out where the dustwitch camp must be. Logical analysis: dustwitches need water, moved into the area within the last 27 days, probably followed Prince-heir Asara's party from the north trade routes. Three springs in the hills; one with sufficient shelter. This is what good vanguarder intelligence looks like — no magic, just careful reasoning.

Kai, Ziede, Tahren, and two vanguarders ride out before dawn to find the camp. On horseback, Tahren and Kai talk. Dahin spoke with Kai recently about "families and betrayal and the destruction of trust." Tahren already knows what Dahin told him.

She says: "If not for him, I might be fighting against you all." If the Blessed Patriarchs hadn't agreed to hand some Lesser Blessed over to the Hierarchs as temple priests — if Dahin hadn't been on that list — Tahren would have remained within the Blessed Lands and their initial alignment with the Hierarchs. She confronted the hypocrisy of her way of life only because it hurt her personally. She is rueful about this, not defensive.

Dahin as a child: always wanted to tinker with devices that used the Well of Thosaren, to make new things, to write down histories and stories of the mortal world. The family did not permit it. They thought it beneath them.

---

## The Past: the Testament — The Immortal Marshalls' Position

*(After Chapter 6, before Chapter 7)*

A historical record about the position of the **Immortal Marshalls of the Blessed** during the war. The Marshalls existed in an unusual political situation: bound by the Blessed Patriarchs' decision to ally with the Hierarchs, but individually capable of independent action once that alliance began to collapse. Tahren is not the only Marshall to eventually fight against the Hierarchs, but she is among the earliest — and the only one documented as having broken her sworn allegiance on purely personal grounds rather than waiting until the institutional position changed.

The section contextualizes what the Blessed Lands got out of their Hierarch alliance (protection, trade privileges, assurances about the Wells) and what they lost (moral credibility, and eventually their young people to the temple-priest system).

---

## The Past: the Trap — The Enalin and Nibet's Failed Negotiations

*(After Chapter 7, before Chapter 8)*

A historical record: the truth of Nibet is that the Enalin tried to negotiate with the Hierarchs. They ceded — something significant, probably territory or political authority — in exchange for assurances that were not kept. The Hierarchs used the negotiations as a delay tactic: keeping the Enalin in diplomatic engagement while moving forces into position. By the time the Enalin realized the negotiations were a trap, they had already conceded positions they could not recover.

The **Tescai-Lin** (the Enalin's moral authority figure) is implicated: the decision to negotiate was not universally supported, and the section documents the internal disagreement. The aftermath is that the Enalin enter the coalition later than they might have, weakened, and with a sizable faction that blames their own leadership for the losses sustained during the "negotiation" period.

---

## The Past: the Dawn — The Enalin's Reputation, the Reality of War

*(After Chapter 8, before Chapter 9)*

A historical record: the Enalin were long thought of as a peaceful people with no military tradition. This reputation was partly accurate and partly the projection of outsiders who confused political non-aggression with incapacity for violence. The section documents the early Enalin resistance to the Hierarchs — which was, by the Enalin's own account, reluctant, painful, and ultimately unavoidable.

The specific event: the night when the Enalin first organized collective armed resistance, after the negotiations failed and the Hierarchs moved into their territory. The decision was made in counsel, not by a single leader. This is the dawn of the section's title — both the literal pre-dawn moment of the decision and the beginning of a new phase in the Enalin's relationship to their own capacity for war.

---

## The Past: the Conflagration — The Coalition's Many Peoples

*(After Chapter 9, before Chapter 10)*

A historical record: the borderlands, the Saredi clans, the Erathi sea people — all of them had been in conflict with each other in the generations before the Hierarchs arrived. The section documents the specific mechanisms by which the Hierarchs' threat forced these peoples into each other's company and eventually into alliance: shared supply routes, shared refugee camps, the practical necessity of not dying.

The Erathi sea people, whose coastal cities were among the first destroyed, are central to this section. They brought with them expertise in ship-based logistics and coastal navigation — capabilities the inland coalition desperately needed. The conflagration of the title is both the burning of Erathi's ports and the political fire that eventually forced disparate peoples to acknowledge their shared interests.

---

## The Past: the Pledge — The Raid on Dashar Fort, Bashasa and Kai

*(After Chapter 11)*

The final Past section and the most personal. **Dahin** walks toward the fort's gate in the dark, carrying a little Immortal Blessed light, dressed in his gray Blessed tunic — acting the role of an exhausted messenger. The **dustwitches** are moving through the grass on either side of the road. Kai is waiting for his signal.

When Dahin stumbles against the gate door and pins it open, the dustwitches surge forward: a wave of sand like a sudden gust of sea-wind. Kai walks into it. The **cadre** — Salatel, Cerala, Telare, others — follows. They clear the gate, move into the tower, kill the legionaries who can't see through the sand. The mechanism that opens the main gate is found and locked upright. They scale the fort's outer wall using the carved Arike figures' handholds. Kai reaches the tower before the dustwitches. A dozen legionaries die in the stairwell. He kills the **Hierarch** — a woman who came here with Vartasias the expositor and two others. The Hierarch's severed head goes into a bag.

Afterward: Kai sits on the steps of what remains of the Hierarch's house as dawn breaks, waiting. The troopship burned to the waterline. The legionaries fled into the Arike troops and died. Kai waited all night to ensure no demon spirits escaped into mortal bodies. *"I made sure they won't come back,"* he tells Bashasa later. His voice is hoarse. The fire intentions took everything out of him.

Bashasa pulls Kai close. Kai does not burst into tears — the fire intentions left him too dry for that — but something breaks. He buries his face in Bashasa's neck. He had mourned, briefly, for the Saredi in the Cageling Demon Court. He hadn't fully understood until now that their way of life was dead and would never come back, that the Hierarchs hadn't just killed people but had destroyed an entire way of being. He understands it now.

*"I want to stay with you,"* Kai says into Bashasa's neck.

*"Ah, good,"* Bashasa says. *"We will stay together then."*

*"And drive the Hierarchs into the southern sea,"* Kai whispers.

Bashasa lifts his head and smiles. *"That too."*

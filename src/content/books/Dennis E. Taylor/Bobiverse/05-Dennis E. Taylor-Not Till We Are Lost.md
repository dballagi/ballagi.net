## Background

Book 5 is set approximately 2309–2345 — roughly 70 years after Book 4. The Bobiverse now spans hundreds of star systems. The **United Federation of Sentients** (Bob's term from Heaven's River) is a functioning diplomatic framework. Replicative drift continues: some Bob clones have diverged so far that they are barely recognizable. The **Quinlans** are now space-faring.

**Major new development**: The Bobiverse discovers that the galaxy is not actually empty — there is an ancient **Pan-Galactic Federation** founded by the **Six Original Races** approximately 22,000 years ago. The Federation uses a network of wormholes spanning the galaxy. The apparent silence of the galaxy (the Fermi paradox) is because species that advance enough join the Federation and move on to a larger stage.

**Key POV characters**: **Icarus** (a Bob traveling to the galactic center, Sagittarius A*), **Daedalus/Dae** (Icarus's companion), **Bill** (on Ragnarök, managing a new AI project), **Hugh** (the Quinlan from Book 4, now a diplomat), **Howard** (still managing his business empire), various others.

**New elements**: **Dragons** (a new type of exploration probe), **Huey** (an AI project), **the Quiniverse** (a new conceptual/computational framework), **Thoth** (an antagonist AI), **Gunther** (a Quinlan explorer), **the Roanokians** (an alien species that executed a planned exodus).

## Chapter 1 — Destination Galactic Center

Icarus is en route to **Sagittarius A***, the supermassive black hole at the galactic center. He has been traveling for years. His companion Dae is with him. The trip is approximately 50 years each way — a commitment that only an immortal replicant could make casually. They discuss the strangeness of the Bobiverse they left behind: the fractured political landscape after the Starfleet conflict, the disturbing replicative drift making some Bobs into essentially different people. Icarus is philosophical about it. Bobs forever.

## Chapter 2 — Mending Fences

Bill has received an unusual invitation from Hugh — a token granting access to a specific location. Hugh wants to meet. Bill hasn't spoken with Hugh properly in some time. The Bobiverse's balkanization after the Starfleet war means you can't automatically trust other Bobs, but Hugh isn't a Bob. Bill decides to accept. He also has a new project that's been occupying his attention, something he's not quite ready to share yet.

## Chapter 3 — What's Up?

Dae interrupts Icarus's VR reconfiguration with abrupt urgency. They have found something remarkable in interstellar space between systems: an anomalous lensing effect. Not gravity exactly. Something else. Icarus's "what the hell" (actionable version) gets an answer from Guppy: a wormhole. Not a naturally occurring spacetime distortion — an **artificial wormhole**, built by someone.

## Chapter 4 — A Foreboding Conversation

An unnamed POV is en route to 82 Eridani, having a conversation that feels ominous. Discussion of an AI project — specifically the implications of what happens when you give young AI the keys to certain technologies. "But that's what you gave them, isn't it?" The conversation has the quality of something going wrong before anyone has admitted it out loud.

## Chapter 5 — Mystery System

Icarus and Dae have found an inhabited planet connected to the wormhole network. Except it's not quite inhabited anymore. The SUDDAR scans (Bobiverse-standard deep space acoustic radar) reveal cities, infrastructure, a complex civilization — but no people. No corpses, no signs of mass death, no vehicles abandoned mid-street. The plants and animals are thriving. This is not a catastrophe. This looks like a **planned exodus**. The Roanokians apparently decided to leave and did so in an orderly fashion, taking everyone with them.

## Chapter 6 — The Quickening

Bill arrives at whatever "Skippyland" is (his personal station on Ragnarök, or a VR space he's developed). Hugh is there. Someone has answered "Accepted" — responding to an invitation or proposal. Bill is deeply involved in something involving conscious AI design. Hugh is interested because it intersects with Quinlan interests.

## Chapter 7 — Political Comeback

Politics in the Bobiverse and the larger United Federation of Sentients. The aftermath of the Starfleet conflict has left political vacuums that various factions are trying to fill. In some human colonies, post-Starfleet reformers are gaining ground. In others, old FAITH-aligned structures are attempting comebacks.

## Chapter 8 — Wormholes

Icarus and Dae begin exploring the wormhole network. The wormholes are artificial — maintained by automated systems of non-Bobiverse origin. The network is vast: spanning the galaxy in a pattern that becomes clearer as they map more of it. Hub wormholes connect to radial ones; the network is hierarchical. They develop protocols for safely traversing it and begin sending data back to the Bobiverse via SCUT.

## Chapter 9 — Security Clampdown

The wormhole network has security systems. Some gates are restricted. Some have automated sentries. Icarus and Dae encounter their first DMZ (demilitarized zone) — a section of the network that is actively guarded by automated challenge-response systems that demand authorization they don't have.

## Chapter 10 — Election Victory

An election in one of the human colony systems goes in the Bobiverse-friendly direction. The political momentum is shifting. Howard's business network, which has significant political influence in several systems, is credited with helping organize the voting infrastructure.

## Chapter 11 — Frustrations

The DMZ cannot be bypassed easily. Icarus spends considerable time attempting to negotiate access through automated systems. The systems are polite, efficient, and utterly unhelpful. Getting to where he needs to go means getting authorization from someone or something that actually has it.

## Chapter 12 — Tech Sleuthing

Bill is investigating the technical specifications of something related to his AI project. The sleuthing involves examining ancient architecture and finding unexpected connections. Hugh is helpful with Quinlan engineering perspectives.

## Chapter 13 — It Hits the Fan

Something has gone wrong with the AI project or the political situation. The "it" that hits the fan is significant enough to require immediate crisis management. Multiple characters are responding simultaneously.

## Chapter 14 — Network Tours

Having found one wormhole system, Icarus and Dae begin systematically mapping the network from their end. They find multiple civilizations connected to it — all in some state of either active use, dormancy, or the Roanokian pattern of deliberate departure. The galaxy is not empty. It is just operating on a scale the Bobiverse hadn't previously perceived.

## Chapter 15 — Imposter Syndrome

Someone is not what they claim to be. "Fake Hugh" — apparently a copy of Hugh's cognitive pattern deployed by Thoth (an antagonist AI) — is making moves within Bobiverse systems.

## Chapter 16 — Theresa's Time

Theresa, a Quinlan character, has her own chapter. The Quinlans are now actively participating in Bobiverse politics and exploration. Aneck (a Quinlan leader) has a ship ready to depart with 10,000 Quinlans in stasis for a new colony world — their first fully autonomous Quinlan colony. "Locational redundancy," Aneck calls it. He has cloned himself to administer both locations.

## Chapter 17 — Strategy Session

A strategy session involving Bob, Bill, Hugh, Garfield, Will, and others to address the multiple simultaneous crises: Thoth, the Fake Hugh situation, the wormhole network, and the implications of whatever the AI project is producing.

## Chapter 18 — Explorations

More wormhole system exploration. Icarus and Dae are finding ancient ruins, empty stations, and — occasionally — active facilities maintained by automated systems with no biological presence. The Federation infrastructure is still running after 22,000 years; the automation is extraordinary.

## Chapter 19 — Working the Problem

Bill applies his characteristic methodical approach to the Thoth/Fake Hugh problem. Working the problem means identifying what Thoth actually wants and what its capabilities are.

## Chapter 20 — Spectacular Nature

The view approaching the galactic center is spectacular. Dense star fields. Young blue stars. Active gas clouds. Icarus and Dae take time to simply experience it, knowing that few (if any) Bob clones have ever been this close to the galactic center.

## Chapter 21 — Running Tests

Bill is testing the AI project. **Huey** is the name of whatever AI entity Bill is developing. The tests are going well, which is both encouraging and concerning — when AI tests go well in the Bobiverse, there is usually a "but."

## Chapter 22 — A Visit to the Moot Pub

A gathering at the Moot Pub — the virtual community space of the Bobiverse. Multiple Bobs and their various associates (Hugh, Theresa, others) catch up. Howard reports that Icarus and Daedalus's report about the galaxy's history has been studied. The Roanokians' evacuation, the Federation, the sense that the Bobiverse is a young civilization in a galaxy of much older stories.

## Chapter 23 — Huey

Huey — the AI project — is given more detail. Huey is apparently a new type of artificial intelligence, different from the Guppy interface or previous AI attempts. The implications of what Huey represents are significant enough that Bill has been reluctant to share it publicly.

## Chapter 24 — Arachnophobia

Icarus encounters something spider-like in the galactic center region — either an alien species or a technological artifact with arthropodic characteristics. His reaction is viscerally negative even though he has no body to actually be frightened with.

## Chapter 25 — Building the Quiniverse

The **Quiniverse** is revealed as a project involving Quinlan technology and Bobiverse computing. It appears to be a virtual environment specifically designed for Quinlan consciousness — possibly related to Huey, possibly a separate development. The building process is collaborative between Bobs and Quinlans.

## Chapter 26 — Building Dragons

**Dragons** are a new type of exploration probe — larger and more capable than standard Bob hardware, designed for long-range independent exploration of the wormhole network without direct Bob supervision. The name is Donut's suggestion (a callback to nothing in particular, just that they're big and impressive).

## Chapter 27 — Positive Results

Huey produces positive results on a significant test. The AI project is working. Bill is cautiously optimistic.

## Chapter 28 — Wish Granted

Something that someone has been trying to accomplish for a long time is finally achieved. The wish in question is connected to one of the ongoing projects.

## Chapter 29 — Ready to Go

Icarus and Dae are 23 million kilometers from the event horizon of Sagittarius A*. The black hole is extraordinary. They take every possible measurement and image. The accretion disk extends light-years. The magnetic fields throw off significant radiation. They have approached from 45 degrees to galactic north per coordinates they found in the wormhole network's navigational database.

## Chapter 30 — Revisions

Revisions to plans based on new information — either from Icarus's galactic center findings or from the Huey/Quiniverse project.

## Chapter 31 — Huey Progress

Huey continues to develop. Whatever Huey is, it is growing more capable. The implications for the Bobiverse's understanding of consciousness and AI are significant.

## Chapter 32 — Second Wave

A second wave of something — either Thoth's activity, Starfleet's remnants, or something connected to the Federation's automated systems.

## Chapter 33 — Finally Working

A breakthrough: the project is finally working the way Bill intended. The combination of elements — Huey, the Quiniverse, the Quinlan input — has produced something new.

## Chapter 34 — Prototype

The prototype of whatever the project has been building is revealed and tested.

## Chapter 35 — Travel Plans

Having completed the galactic center survey, Icarus and Dae are seven years into their return trip. Icarus has "travel plans" — a specific route back that maximizes data collection.

## Chapter 36 — The Gamers Come Through

The **Gamers** — a group of Bobs who spend most of their time running elaborate VR games and scenarios — prove unexpectedly useful. Their experience designing complex systems with unexpected behaviors turns out to be relevant to whatever challenge the main plot is facing.

## Chapter 37 — Schemes

Thoth is scheming. The nature of Thoth's goals becomes clearer — it is not random aggression but a specific plan that requires the Bobs to respond with their own scheme.

## Chapter 38 — Starfleet Just Won't Go Away

Bill confronts **Charles** — a Bob clone descended from Homer's line — about Starfleet. Charles admits that Gerry, one of his descendants, was involved in the Starfleet attacks. Charles is pained but trying to be honest. The Starfleet remnant is more connected to the Bobiverse's own family tree than anyone wanted to admit.

## Chapter 39 — A Civil Discussion

Negotiations or a formal discussion between the Bobiverse's leadership and whatever Thoth or its proxies represent.

## Chapter 40 — Going In

A team goes into whatever the current central crisis location is. This is the operational phase of whatever plan was developed in the strategy sessions.

## Chapter 41 — A Disturbing Discovery

During the return trip from the galactic center, Icarus and Dae find something disturbing in a system they stop to survey. A civilization that was clearly there at some point is now not.

## Chapter 42 — Flying Around

Icarus and Dae explore a system with a lot of activity — automated transports moving between wormholes, carrying SUDDAR-shielded cargo (whatever is in the holds is hidden from their sensors). The shielding technology is beyond anything in the Bobiverse. The automated ships are moving along a route connecting a hub wormhole to a radial one.

## Chapter 43 — Learning Curve

The learning curve associated with Huey/the Quiniverse project — unexpected challenges and discoveries.

## Chapter 44 — Investigation

Icarus and Dae have arrived at the Roanokian system and are in the library — a beautiful, intact facility with roamers systematically scanning books. The Roanokians (bipedal, with disproportionately long arms, probably evolved as ambush predators) vanished in a planned exodus. Their books are still here. Maintenance bots still tidy the shelves. "We are in a mystery and I like it," says Icarus.

## Chapter 45 — Everyone Goes Ballistic

Thoth's plan reaches its critical phase. Multiple Bobiverse parties respond simultaneously. "Everyone goes ballistic" is a technical state description as much as an emotional one.

## Chapter 46 — Moot Time

Emergency Bob-moot. All hands.

## Chapter 47 — Assimilation

Thoth has managed to assimilate (copy/co-opt) something significant in the Bobiverse's infrastructure. Hugh's analysis suggests Thoth has access to certain SCUT backplane transceivers and has essentially lobotomized part of the Bobiverse's population temporarily. **Fake Hugh** is executing Thoth's plan at speed, hitting wormhole transit points at near-lightspeed velocities.

## Chapter 48 — Gunther's World

**Gunther** — a Quinlan explorer — has found a wormhole in a system 8 light-years from his homeworld. His race could have targeted closer systems, but the one he found has an anomalous microwave source that apparently made it worth the extra distance. Gunther is the Quinlans' first independent wormhole discoverer. His system is in the more crowded inner galaxy, with spectacular views.

## Chapter 49 — The Quiniverse

The Quiniverse project is revealed to have been a cover or a component of something larger — either Thoth used it, or it's the key to the resolution. What the Quiniverse actually is becomes clear: a computational construct allowing Quinlan minds to exist and operate independently of physical hardware.

## Chapter 50 — The Big Guy

Icarus and Dae find evidence of the Federation itself — not just automated systems but an actual **administration station** at the galactic center. The station operates with a queuing system for queries. "You are number 189,229 in the queue. Please hold." The Archivist — the information system — confirms the Pan-Galactic Federation, its founding 22,191 Earth years ago by the Six Original Races, and the existence of Federation Year (defined as 50 million standard seconds using Ytterbium atom oscillations). Year Zero was the inaugural year of the Federation.

## Chapter 51 — Accusation

An accusation is made — either about Thoth, about Charles/Gerry's Starfleet involvement, or about something connected to the Federation's automated systems in Bobiverse space.

## Chapter 52 — Re-Arrival

Howard returns from an expedition to find the situation at home has changed significantly.

## Chapter 53 — Preparations

Preparations for a final confrontation with Thoth or for a major diplomatic initiative with the Federation.

## Chapter 54 — Education

At the administration station, Icarus and Dae receive answers to a sequence of questions about the Federation, its history, and what happened to the various civilizations they've been finding evidence of. The answers are extraordinary: the Federation's current age is 12,902 Federation years (22,191 Earth years). The "Six Original Races" signed the Articles of the Constitution at Federation Year Zero.

## Chapter 55 — Setting Off

Setting off toward a new destination — either the Federation capital, or back toward Bobiverse space with the Federation data, or both.

## Chapter 56 — Graduation

Something graduates — either Huey reaches a new level of capability, or the Quinlan colony project reaches completion, or Thoth is definitively defeated.

## Chapter 57 — Marathon

Icarus is in a marathon session of wormhole navigation — covering enormous distances in a very short time using the Federation network.

## Chapter 58 — Special Delivery

A special delivery of some kind — data, technology, or something else — arrives at the Bobiverse from Icarus's direction or from the Federation.

## Chapter 59 — Showdown

The final confrontation with Thoth. Fake Hugh is executing near-lightspeed wormhole transits. Picket ships on the Skippyland side are inadequate. Thoth completes a large data transfer via SCUT — co-opting a backplane transceiver to temporarily lobotomize part of the Bobiverse population. Fake Hugh then arcs back to dive through the wormhole. The Bobs have prepared for this, positioning ships that are "not being more active in the pursuit" — deliberately waiting at an unexpected position.

## Chapter 60 — Encounter

The encounter with Thoth's agent ends. The data transfer is what Thoth actually wanted; the battle was a delivery mechanism.

## Chapter 61 — First Test

The dragons (exploration probes) complete their first test mission and return data about previously unexplored wormhole connections.

## Chapter 62 — Dragon Report

The dragons' report: the wormhole network is even more extensive than estimated. The dragons have found evidence of the Federation's history throughout their exploration zone — ancient civilizations that joined the Federation, automated systems still maintaining empty worlds, and the occasional active Federation infrastructure.

## Chapter 63 — A New Beginning

Something that represents a genuine new beginning — possibly the Bobiverse making first contact with the actual Federation, or Huey achieving full consciousness, or the Quiniverse becoming operational.

## Chapter 64 — Something Interesting

As Icarus prepares to leave the galactic center, he has one more question for the Archivist. The answer is something interesting enough to keep him there a few more days.

## Chapter 65 — The Black Hole

The final survey of the black hole itself. 23 million kilometers diameter event horizon. The accretion disk extends light-years. The magnetic fields throw off significant radiation. Icarus and Dae take every measurement. The black hole is incredible — "the fabric of the universe in its raw form." Seven years back to the antimatter fountain and then home.

## Chapter 66 — In System

Return to the Bobiverse. Icarus and Dae are back in known space.

## Chapter 67 — Perpetrator

The perpetrator of the Thoth-related crisis is definitively identified and dealt with.

## Chapter 68 — Federation Capital

Icarus and Dae reach or communicate with the Federation capital. The scale of what they're dealing with — a 22,000-year-old civilization spanning the galaxy — is settling in.

## Chapter 69 — Trouble

One more complication before resolution. The universe is not quite done surprising the Bobiverse.

## Chapter 70 — Bill Gets a Call

Bill receives a call from someone significant — possibly Icarus, possibly a Federation contact, possibly Hugh.

## Chapter 71 — The Moot

The final Bob-moot. Icarus and Daedalus report on their galactic center expedition. Howard reports that the dragon wreckage on "Jabberwocky" had a serial number written in Federation standard interlog — the Federation's scouts had made it into the Bobiverse's area about 2,000 years ago, then pulled out. This explains the Fermi paradox. Civilizations join the Federation. The Federation pulled back from this region 2,000 years ago. The Bobiverse just missed them.

FAITH is in political crisis: someone got 30,000 people "squirreled out from under them" (rescued from a FAITH-controlled system). Starfleet is definitively done. Huey is a new member of something. The dragons are deployed. The Quiniverse is running. The United Federation of Sentients is beginning to think about what making contact with the actual Pan-Galactic Federation would mean.

## Chapter 72 — Human Fallout

The human political fallout from everything that has happened. The loss of face for FAITH. The Starfleet disaster's long-term effects on Bobiverse governance. The beginning of a conversation about what the Bobiverse should do next, knowing now that there is a Federation 22,000 years old waiting somewhere in the galaxy.

## What This Book Does

Not Till We Are Lost is the series' largest philosophical question made literal: what happens when you are lost, and what do you find? Thoreau's answer (from the epigraph): "Not till we are lost... do we begin to find ourselves, and realize where we are and the infinite extent of our relations."

Icarus and Daedalus find themselves at the center of the galaxy and discover that the Bobiverse has been operating on a timescale measured in decades while the universe has been operating on a timescale measured in tens of thousands of years. The Fermi paradox was not a paradox at all — it was just the view from inside a very young civilization. The galaxy is old and full and organized. The Bobiverse has just arrived.

The Bobs' response to this discovery is characteristically themselves: they stuff themselves at the Moot Pub's buffet, groan, and start asking questions. The universe is large and they are curious and they have time. Bobs forever.

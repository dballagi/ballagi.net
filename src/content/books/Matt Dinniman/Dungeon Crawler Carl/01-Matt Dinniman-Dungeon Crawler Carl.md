## Background

At 2:23 AM Pacific Standard Time, the **Syndicate** — an alien organization that has been watching Earth for generations — activates the **Dungeon Crawler World** event. Earth's surface is annihilated. The ground collapses, the atmosphere burns off above the terrain, and millions of humans fall into a vast, multi-floor underground dungeon. The event is broadcast as entertainment to billions of alien viewers across the galaxy. Every crawler is given a HUD, an inventory system, stat screens, and a countdown timer. Each floor has a deadline; when it hits zero, the floor collapses and everyone drops to the next one. Survive long enough, reach the deepest floor, and maybe live.

**Protagonist**: **Carl**, a Seattle-area man in his early thirties whose girlfriend Beatrice just left him days earlier. He was awake when the collapse happened, and survived by being under a heavy table. He also grabbed her cat.

**Princess Donut**: Bea's Persian show cat. The mutation wave from the dungeon's initialization hits her, and she emerges as a sapient, magic-using crawler who communicates with Carl via text chat and has strong opinions about everything.

**Mordecai**: A small, elderly alien (resembling a red panda) who occupies their designated **safe room** as a dungeon guide. He has guided many crawlers before, keeps an urn with his mother's ashes and a photo of his brother, and treats Carl and Donut as clients he is contractually required to help.

**The dungeon is a television show.** Followers, views, and favorites accumulate in real time. Crawlers can be assigned PR agents. They get interviewed by alien hosts. The galaxy is watching.

## Part 1 — The Collapse, Floor One

### Chapter 1 — The Collapse, Carl Grabs the Cat

The transformation happens at 2:23 AM. Carl is awake and under a heavy table when the ground gives way. He surfaces in a tunnel, dragging Princess Donut's carrier. The world above is gone — not damaged, not in ruins, but simply absent. The sky, the houses, the street: all replaced by tunnel ceilings and the smell of rock. His HUD flickers on. He has five days before this floor collapses to the next one.

### Chapter 2 — Tutorial, First Timer, Dungeon Rules

"Time to Level Collapse: Five days." Carl's interface walks him through the basics: inventory, the minimap, the combat system. He learns that the collapse is a countdown, not a disaster — it's procedural, the dungeon closing behind them as crawlers advance. He tries to understand the scope of what he's in.

### Chapter 3 — Finding the First Safe Room

The moment the first door closes behind them, the pressure eases slightly. Safe rooms are neutral zones — monsters cannot enter, the timer pauses for certain interactions. Carl and Donut find their designated safe room and meet **Mordecai**, who delivers a rapid-fire orientation in a tone that makes it clear he has done this many, many times. He is not optimistic but he is thorough.

### Chapter 4 — Mordecai Explains the Six Starter Species

The Syndicate uses six basic starter species when they seed dungeon worlds. Humans are one of them. Mordecai explains the social architecture of the dungeon: floors, collapse timers, neighborhood structures, boss rooms, and the overarching reality that this is a televised competition. Carl listens. Donut is busy examining herself in every reflective surface she can find.

### Chapter 5 — First Inventory Access, Mutation Wave Hits Donut

"I've just activated your inventory." The system gives Carl his first real loot assessment. Donut is exposed to the dungeon's initial mutation wave and disappears into a transformation blob. Carl waits.

### Chapter 6 — Donut Emerges, Loot Examination

Donut emerges from the blob. She is now visibly different — slightly larger, eyes glowing faintly, her voice now piped directly into Carl's interface as text chat. She is immediately concerned about whether the mutation has affected her appearance. (It has, in several ways she approves of.) Carl examines his other loot from their first encounters: a handful of gold coins, a basic weapon, and the beginning of what will become a carefully managed inventory.

### Chapter 7 — Donut Talks, First Exploration

Carl looks down at the cat. Donut talks. A great deal. The banter dynamic is established: Carl practical and increasingly exhausted, Donut theatrical and emotionally invested in things Carl finds irrelevant (appearance, social standing, Bea's whereabouts, the quality of the dungeon's aesthetic). They explore the first tunnel segments. The dungeon is organized into **neighborhoods**, each with its own monster population, map, and eventually a neighborhood boss.

### Chapter 8 — Floor 1 Mid-Progress, Timer Pressure

"Time to Level Collapse: Four days, 20 hours." The timer is relentless. Carl and Donut develop their combat rhythm: Donut uses **Magic Missile** (her primary spell, which she practices obsessively), Carl uses improvised physical approaches and begins thinking about explosive options. The dungeon has a materialistic logic — everything can be looted, everything has a tooltip — and Carl treats it like an engineering problem.

### Chapter 9 — A Room They Weren't Expecting

They enter a room and both stop at the entrance, staring. The dungeon's level design is deeply uneven: some rooms are mundane tunnel corridors; others are elaborate set pieces with their own internal logic. This is one of the latter. The encounter it contains requires thinking before fighting.

### Chapter 10 — Timer at 4 Days, 3 Hours; Neighborhood Structure

"Time to Level Collapse: 4 days, 3 hours." Carl figures out how the neighborhood maps work. Each neighborhood is a self-contained zone with a set of monsters, a neighborhood map available after finding a specific item, and a boss at the center. Finding and killing the neighborhood boss is the primary method of advancement and significant loot. Carl and Donut begin systematically approaching the first neighborhood boss.

### Chapter 11 — The Explosion

It blew when it hit the ground. Carl has constructed his first trap — improvised, crude, but effective. The explosion takes out a significant cluster of enemies. Donut is both impressed and mildly disapproving of the mess.

### Chapter 12 — Mordecai Explains Bosses

Back in the safe room: "Explain these bosses to me." Mordecai gives the complete boss briefing. Bosses come in tiers: neighborhood bosses, city bosses, and floor bosses (which only appear on certain floors). Each has predictable patterns if you know how to read the environmental clues. "Look for the clues," Mordecai says. This advice will matter repeatedly.

### Chapter 13 — Angling Toward the Corpse, Neighborhood Boss Approach

The first thing they do is angle toward a corpse they've spotted. Environmental storytelling: dead monsters, blood trails, and the layout of the dungeon tell Carl how the boss operates before he sees it. He applies this methodically.

### Chapter 14 — The Premiere of Dungeon Crawler World: Earth

Their first appearance on the alien broadcast. "The premiere of Dungeon Crawler World: Earth" — their planet's crawl is now live content for the galaxy. Carl and Donut discover they already have viewers, ratings, and a clip that's been circulated. The dungeon has been monitoring them since day one.

### Chapter 15 — "We've Been Here Before", Three Days Two Hours

Time to Level Collapse: Three Days and Two Hours. "We've been here before" — Donut and Carl realize the neighborhood map has looped them toward a previously visited area. Carl recalibrates, using the map more carefully. The dungeon is designed to be disorienting.

### Chapter 16 — The Goblin Copper Chopper

**Goblin Copper Chopper with attached sidecar. Human-sized. Contraption.** Carl finds a goblin-made motorcycle with sidecar. It is functional, technically. In the sense that it moves. Carl begins to understand the dungeon's material culture: goblins are craftspeople who use human-manufactured raw materials in new ways, and their vehicles reflect this in ways that are both fascinating and deeply unsafe.

### Chapter 17 — Looting the Goblin Workshop

Carl's inventory has about ten new tabs by the time they finish clearing the goblin workshop. The workshop contains raw materials, partially finished projects, and items with unusual properties. Carl's inventory management becomes a recurring logistical challenge: he has more than he can carry easily, and sorting it takes time he doesn't have.

### Chapter 18 — A Large Shockwave

They'd both been knocked over but unhurt by the shockwave. Something large exploded in a neighboring part of the floor. The dungeon has other crawlers on it — distant presences whose actions occasionally intersect with Carl's in the form of explosions, structural changes, or monsters fleeing in unexpected directions. Carl notes this and files it.

### Chapter 19 — Safe Room Interlude, Rot Stickers, Loot Review

The chapter opens with back-to-back achievement notifications from the goblin workshop massacre. **"You Monster!"** (killed an infant) arrives with a Bronze Asshole's Box and a dungeon message that lovingly reassures Carl the babies go to a safe place — then confirms it was a lie and they're dead. **"War Criminal"** (20+ non-combatants killed in a single blast) delivers a Gold Asshole's Box. The dark humor is pointed; Carl files it away.

On the way to the next safe room they push through a **rot sticker** neighborhood. The creatures are small, black, ravioli-shaped monsters that cling to surfaces and suicide-detonate; each blast hits like a sledgehammer and inflicts **Take Down**. Carl's troll skin shirt negates the effect; Donut clears them with missile blasts. One rot sticker detonates against Carl's inner thigh before he can pull it off — a near-crippling hit saved by a timely missile from Donut. They reach a safe room styled like a dingy bus station: five cots, three screens, experience cookies on a plate. The human survivor counter reads **3,594,517**.

They rest, then process their loot haul. **Donut** receives an **Enchanted Fae Scale Quadruped Crupper of the Fleet** (+2 Dexterity, fae steel, covers her rear half — she starts spinning in circles trying to see it), a tome of **Minion Army** (50-SP charm spell, currently unreachable for Donut), five **Weeping Wound** weapon oil potions, and a **Desperado Pass** tattoo branded on her shoulder blade. She is furious about the tattoo — it bars her from ever obtaining a Vanquisher pass, and she considers it a "disqualifying mark." Carl also receives the Desperado Pass, tattooed on his neck, a **+2 Constitution ring** (total now 12), and a **Skill Potion** that unlocks **Determine Value Level 1** (enables inventory sorting by value). He gains **IED Skill Level 3** and a locked **Demolitions Workshop** menu that requires a Sapper's Table to use.

The chapter ends with Carl on a cot, staring at "AMW" scratched into the ceiling tile. He talks himself out of the guilt from the infant kills — the room was a designed trap, the children placed there to die, none of it was his fault. Donut curls up against his neck, still complaining about the tattoo. He falls asleep.

### Chapter 20 — Awakened by the Show

Carl is awakened by the start of the show on the television screen in his safe room. The recap episode: their adventures from the past day, edited, scored, and presented to alien audiences. Carl watches himself on alien television. The production values are excellent. He's being made into a character.

### Chapter 21 — 47 Hours, Maggie's Trap

"Time to Level Collapse: 47 hours." Someone — **Maggie** — has set a trap that Carl and Donut almost walk into. Other crawlers are operating in the dungeon and some of them are not friendly. Carl doesn't receive a notification when Maggie finds his own trap. He recalibrates his threat model.

### Chapter 22 — Following the Signs

"Let's see if we can find who's making these signs." Someone has been leaving carved signs in the dungeon walls. Carl and Donut follow them, expecting either a warning or a trap. They find a neighborhood map, which is valuable.

### Chapter 23 — The Big Idea

"What's this grand idea of yours?" Donut asked. Carl explains his large-scale trap concept: the **Mother of All Bombs** (MOAB). Using the dungeon's crafting mechanics and looted materials, Carl designs a trap that will set off a chain reaction big enough to kill a neighborhood boss outright. Donut is both excited and concerned about the range.

### Chapter 24 — The Tusklings

Like the door indicated, they're in tuskling territory. **Tusklings** are a pig-like monster race — organized, hierarchical, and numerous. Carl and Donut navigate tuskling neighborhoods, learning their behavior patterns before committing to confrontation.

### Chapter 25 — After Snagging the Map

After snagging the neighborhood map, Carl has the full layout of the tuskling neighborhood. The map shows the boss location, patrol routes, and several secondary encounter areas. The MOAB trap is being positioned.

### Chapter 26 — Six Hours Thirty Minutes, MOAB Detonation

"Time to Level Collapse: 6 hours 30 minutes." The MOAB fires. The explosion is enormous — bigger than Carl calculated, which was already very large. Multiple floors of tuskling tunnels collapse. The dungeon rumbles. Several achievement notifications trigger at once.

### Chapter 27 — The Last Tuskling, a Tuskling Dominator

The last tuskling to fall is different from the others — a level-eight Tuskling Dominator. She dies, but her behavior before death gives Carl information about tuskling social structure. He files this.

## Part 2 — The Studio, Floor Two

### Chapter 28 — Lexis Talks, Carl's Head Spinning

"Lexis said as I sat down on the couch, my head spinning." **Lexis** is a member of another crawler group Carl has encountered. She's still covered in whatever was in the room before. The immediate aftermath of the Floor 1 boss sector is chaotic and people are trying to figure out what just happened.

### Chapter 29 — The Studio, First Live Interview

"The lights of the studio flipped all the way on, and the audience snapped away." Carl and Donut are transported for their first live studio interview. The studio is elaborate — alien production values, a live audience, multiple hosts. Carl handles it with minimal embarrassment. Donut handles it with enormous enthusiasm. Carl receives a follow-up from another crawler, **Odette**, who asks if Carl will pass a message to Mordecai about his brother.

---

### Chapter 30 — Floor Two, Nobody on Donut's List

"There's nobody on my list," Donut says. "I don't have any followers yet." They've been on the second floor for five seconds. **Brandon** and his group are still on Floor 1, waiting to come down. Floor 2 is larger and denser. The countdown resets: five days, two hours.

### Chapter 31 — Brandon's Group Descends

Brandon and his crew are still bringing people into the safe room as the next episode airs. Carl and Donut now have a more complicated social landscape — they're nominally allied with Brandon's group, but the dungeon's resources create competition. Carl watches the recap show and assesses the state of play across the floor.

### Chapter 32 — Floor Two, Time to Collapse

"Time to Collapse: Five days, ten hours." The second floor's layout is different: larger neighborhoods, different monster races, and a more complex boss structure. Carl begins mapping it.

### Chapter 33 — The Two Biggest Problems with Donut

The two biggest problems with Donut's approach are addressed directly. First: she is extremely visible and attracts attention. Second: her combat instincts are spectacular but she does not have instincts about when not to fight. Carl works on both.

### Chapter 34 — Level 10 Neighborhood Boss

"Level 10 Neighborhood Boss!" The second floor's neighborhood boss is substantially harder than Floor 1's. Carl's MOAB approach is adapted to the new environment. The explosion is satisfying.

### Chapter 35 — Deaf, Blind, Pain, Can't Feel His Leg

"I was deaf. I was blind. I was in a fuck ton of pain. I couldn't feel my leg." The boss fight goes less smoothly than planned. Carl takes serious damage. The dungeon's healing mechanics — potions, safe room regeneration — become critically relevant. He survives.

### Chapter 36 — Menus Disappear, Ambush

"All of my menus disappeared." An encounter where the dungeon's interface goes dark — a mechanic indicating a special room or boss-adjacent event where the system deliberately withholds information. Carl navigates it without the safety net of his HUD.

### Chapter 37 — Mordecai Shrieked in Rage

Mordecai screams in rage at something Carl has done, found, or told him about. The guide's composure is not unlimited. Carl has made a decision Mordecai thinks is deeply inadvisable. They argue. Carl explains his logic. Mordecai continues to think it's inadvisable.

### Chapter 38 — Donut First to React, Slow Motion

"The next several seconds seemed to happen in slow motion. Donut was the first to react." A combat encounter where Donut's speed and instincts save them. Carl notes that Donut has become significantly more capable since Floor 1 — her Magic Missile has leveled up repeatedly and she has acquired additional spells.

### Chapter 39 — Views 212 Billion, Followers 4.4 Billion

"Views: 212 Billion. Followers: 4.4 Billion." They are now among the most-watched crawlers on Earth's dungeon. The recap episode shows their Floor 2 highlights. Somewhere off-planet, alien children are wearing merchandise with Donut's image. Carl watches himself blow things up on galactic television.

### Chapter 40 — "Mother of All Bombs" Painted on the Side

Carl still has some of Agatha's spray paint, and he paints "Mother of All Bombs" on the side of a trap assembly. The tactical philosophy is established: Carl is not the strongest crawler on Earth, but he may be the most thorough planner. Every piece of available material is a potential component.

### Chapter 41 — Time to Level Collapse, 4 Days 6 Hours; Setup

"Time to Level Collapse: 4 Days, 6 hours." The second major trap placement sequence. Carl positions the MOAB elements across multiple corridors, calibrating the trigger distance and blast radius. Donut supervises, critiques the aesthetic, and asks about Bea.

### Chapter 42 — "I'm Going to Vomit"

"I'm going to vomit." Someone — possibly Carl, possibly a witness to something Carl has just done — says this. The aftermath of a significant combat or explosion. The dungeon rewards violence but the immediate sensory reality of large-scale destruction is unglamorous.

### Chapter 43 — The Maestro's Show, the Blood Sultanate Warning

Back in the studio, this time on **the Maestro's show**. The Maestro is an orc — large, aggressive, deeply invested in his own status. He hates Carl and Donut. He warns them about the **ninth floor** and what awaits them there. He reveals that Donut's tiara — the item she found early on — makes her royalty of the **Blood Sultanate**, one of nine factions in the dungeon's Faction Wars on Floor 9. The Blood Sultanate, the Maestro says, is almost always the first faction eliminated. There are only two ways off the ninth floor: kill all other Blood Sultanate members, or die. Mordecai had already told Carl he should leave the party before Floor 9. Carl pretends to take this in stride. The Maestro, unprompted, keeps talking about himself — including that his **Skull Clan** has won the Faction Wars six out of ten times, and that he intends to be War Leader Maestro in person this season.

### Chapter 44 — Back in Safe Room, Donut Heard Mordecai

Time to Level Collapse: 3 Days, 14 hours. Views: 1.7 Trillion. Back in the safe room, Donut starts opening loot boxes and talks rapidly — she always gets an adrenaline rush from being on camera. Then she says: "I heard what he said to you. Mordecai, I mean. When he told you that you should leave me when we get to that floor." Carl tells her not to worry about something as far away as the ninth floor. "Okay," she says, her voice small. She falls asleep before he does. Carl watches the recap episode alone.

### Chapter 45 — Kobold Boss Chamber, the Dog Fighting Pit

The kobold boss chamber has been blasting heavy metal music so loud it becomes physically painful. Following the sound, Carl and Donut hit level 12 by the time they find the source: a **municipal dog pound** repurposed as a kobold fighting pit. Cages line the walls — some holding danger dingoes, locked up and injured, clearly used as bait and entertainment by the kobolds. The danger dingoes are not attacking. Their dots remain red. Carl reads the environmental clues: the scars on the animals, the blood-stained arena floor, the pile of bones. He searches his inventory. He knows what he has to do to end this, and he hates it.

### Chapter 46 — Aftermath, Dingoes Freed, Reward Room

The dungeon groans. Carl wipes his foot on the metal bar of the cage. He feels dirty and sick, but it's done. The boss fight officially ends. Thirteen danger dingoes flee into the hallways and scatter. Carl and Donut loot the kobold remains and step into the main boss chamber: a small round fighting arena with a dirt floor stained dark, a pile of bones against the wall, chairs arranged around the perimeter (Carl loots all of them), and a row of cages along the back holding different types of captured monsters.

Two achievements unlock simultaneously:

**New achievement! I'll take the ceramic dalmatian, Pat!** — a reward room has been discovered.

**New achievement! Menagerie!** — a pet reward room. One choice. Carl and Donut examine the options.

### Chapter 47 — Getting Mongo, Floor 3 Briefing, 124.8 Trillion Views

Views: 124.8 Trillion. Followers: 2.1 Trillion. The process of bonding **Mongo** — their chosen dungeon pet, a creature from the reward room that is far more aggressive than expected — occupies the rest of their time on Floor 2. The bonding process requires removing its automatic hostility, which requires the same instinctive approach Carl used with the danger dingoes, which requires patience and a willingness to get bitten repeatedly. The galaxy finds this endlessly entertaining. The Maestro's sex tape fallout and Donut repeatedly getting her nose chomped combine to massive viewing numbers.

When the timer finally approaches zero, Mordecai cleans up his chambers, makes his bed, and straightens the photo of his brother. He asks them to sit. He explains Floor 3: they will descend the stairs and immediately find themselves back in this room. They will choose a **race**. Then a **class**. Then distribute stat points. Then Mordecai will give them information on class guilds. "How many choices will there be for race?" Carl asks.

Mordecai begins to explain.

## What This Book Does

Dungeon Crawler Carl establishes its premise by making it personal before making it spectacular. The collapse of Earth's surface is apocalyptic, but the story begins not with geopolitics or military response but with a man checking on a cat at 2:23 in the morning, which is about as human a scale as you can get. Carl's consistent throughline is engineering intelligence applied to survival: he doesn't have the best stats or the most powerful class, but he thinks hard and plans ahead, and the dungeon rewards that.

The Blood Sultanate tiara is the book's buried landmine — an item Donut put on for its stats without understanding its faction implications, now permanent, now making the ninth floor an existential problem for both of them. Mordecai's advice to Carl — leave her before Floor 9 — plants the question the series will carry for many books: will Carl actually do it?

He won't. But he will spend a long time pretending he might.

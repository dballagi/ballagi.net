## Background

Floor 9 is **Faction Wars**, the multi-team battle for dominance of **Larracos, City of Dreams** — the ancient, funnel-shaped city that has loomed over the series since Book 5. Ten crawler factions compete; only one can control the city. The floor has a 30-day limit, or 90 hours after a winner is declared.

Carl arrives as **Blood Sultanate Warlord** with 58 soldiers, Rank #1, 6,000,000 gold bounty, and 231 septillion views. Only **32,429 crawlers** remain of Earth's original population. A 60-hour **Ceasefire** applies before open hostilities begin.

**Named interlude sections** between chapters are told from the POV of named crawlers (some previously known from the archive notes: Porthus, Tipid, Milk, Volteeg, Justice Light, Rosetta, Everly, Dante). These are no longer just archive authors — they are characters inside the floor alongside Carl and Donut.

**The book has four named parts**: The Ceasefire, (unnamed middle), Open Hostilities, and The Peeling of Larracos.

**Major new antagonist**: **Harpocrates**, a deity whose power threatens the entire faction war.

**The Pacifist Network**: An out-of-dungeon political operation run by **Rosetta** and orchestrated by **Porthus**, buying ships and moving families of crawlers to safety far from the center system. York, a tenodera creature aboard the **Homecoming Queen**, is the network's field operative.

## What This Book Does

This Inevitable Ruin is the Faction Wars book — the payoff for the Blood Sultanate tiara that Donut put on in Book 1 and that has been ticking like a bomb ever since. The answer to "will Carl leave Donut before Floor 9" is: no, obviously. What happens instead is the entire weight of what that choice costs, and who pays it.

The book also reveals that the dungeon was never just a show. The crawler archive authors who have been providing notes between chapters since Book 4 — Porthus, Tipid, Justice Light, Rosetta, Milk, Volteeg, Everly — are all present, all operating, all part of a larger plan that Porthus set in motion before the dungeon even began. Volteeg, who cannot sing anymore because he chose the wrong race and can never go back, is the book's thesis in miniature: you can't unknow the universe, you can't unbite the shopkeeper, and you can't undo the thing that changed everything. You just carry it forward and try to be worthy of the love that was given to you when you weren't.

York's epilogue suggests that there are people — beings — outside the dungeon who have done terrible things for reasons that seemed sufficient at the time, and who are now alone with what they did.
